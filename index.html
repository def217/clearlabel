What actually changed, and when

2 August 2026 — in force now. Article 50(1): providers must design AI systems that interact with people so those people are informed they are dealing with an AI, unless it is obvious to a reasonably well-informed person. Article 50(4): deployers must disclose deepfakes, and AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest.

2 December 2026 — soon. Article 50(2) machine-readable marking of synthetic audio, image, video and text. Systems already on the market before 2 August 2026 got a four-month transition under the Digital Omnibus, ending on this date.

2 December 2027 — deferred. The Digital Omnibus, given final Council approval on 29 June 2026, pushed most standalone high-risk (Annex III) obligations back by 16 months. Article 50 transparency was not deferred. This is the part most compliance content still gets wrong.

WHO

Deployers, not just AI vendors

If you switched on an AI agent in Zendesk, Intercom, Gorgias, Tidio or HubSpot, you are a deployer. The duty is yours, not your vendor's.

WHERE

Anyone selling into the EU

The Act follows the user. A US or UK company with EU customers is in scope for the people it serves in the EU.

HOW MUCH

Set by each member state

Penalties are national, not EU-wide. Article 99(4) caps transparency breaches at €15m or 3% of worldwide turnover, whichever is higher.

Questions

Does this apply to me if I only use a normal support chat?

If a human answers, Article 50(1) does not bite. It bites the moment an AI system is what the visitor is actually talking to — including the AI agents that Zendesk, Intercom, Gorgias, Tidio and HubSpot now enable by default on many plans. That is why the scanner reports those vendors as "check required" rather than guessing: only you can confirm whether the AI mode is switched on.

Wasn't the AI Act delayed?

Partly. The Digital Omnibus, approved by the Council on 29 June 2026, deferred most standalone high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and product-embedded ones by 12 months. The Article 50 transparency duties were not deferred and applied from 2 August 2026. The one Article 50 element that moved is the machine-readable marking in 50(2), which gets a transition to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market.

Is "obvious from the circumstances" an escape hatch?

It is narrow. The test is what is obvious to a reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect person, judged in context. A widget labelled with a human first name and a photo is the opposite of obvious. If you are relying on this exemption, write down why — that reasoning is what an authority will ask to see.

What does the scanner actually see?

Only the public HTML of the URL you enter. It fingerprints known vendor scripts and searches visible page copy for disclosure wording in ten EU languages. It cannot open your chat widget, cannot see inside your vendor console, and cannot tell whether your AI mode is on. It will miss widgets injected later by a tag manager. Treat it as a fast first pass, not an audit.

Do you store my URL or scan results?

The URL you type and the result it produces stay in your browser — they are never sent to us, and there is no account. We do count page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which is cookieless, does not fingerprint you and does not track you across sites; we would rather disclose that than claim a purity we do not have. The page fetch itself is routed through a public read-only text proxy because browsers block direct cross-site reads.
