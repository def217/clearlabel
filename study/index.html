We scanned 883 EU websites for Article 50 compliance

Article 50(1) has been binding since 2 August 2026 and machine-readable marking of AI content is due 2 December 2026. We wanted a number rather than an assumption, so we measured. Raw data and the scanner are open.

What we found

Two findings are worth separating, because they point at very different populations.

The chat-widget finding is narrow but sharp. Only 4.9% of readable sites ran a detectable chat or AI widget. But of those that did, 92.3% had no AI-disclosure wording anywhere in the page copy. If the vendor's AI mode is switched on — and every vendor we found ships one — that is the exact gap Article 50(1) addresses.

The content-marking finding is broad and stark. Just 2 sites out of 532 carried C2PA Content Credentials or IPTC synthetic-media metadata. Article 50(2) requires machine-readable marking of AI-generated audio, image, video and text, with a transition ending 2 December 2026. This one is not limited to sites with chatbots — it reaches every site publishing AI-assisted media, which in 2026 is most marketing sites.

Where the widget actually lives

Of the 26 sites with a widget, 18 had nothing on the homepage — the widget only appeared on a contact, help or support page. Any Article 50 audit that checks the homepage alone will under-report by roughly two thirds.

Method

  • Sampling frame. The Tranco top-1M list, filtered to 26 EU ccTLDs, taking the highest-ranked domains per country up to a per-country quota. 703 domains in total. Tranco was chosen because it is a published, reproducible ranking rather than a hand-picked list.
  • Collection. 16 August 2026. Each domain's homepage was fetched; where no vendor was found, up to three country-appropriate contact/help paths were tried (/kontakt, /contacto, /contatti and so on).
  • Detection. Regex fingerprints for 36 conversational-AI vendors against page source, plus AI-disclosure phrase matching in 10 EU languages, plus C2PA and IPTC marking signals. The scanner is open source; the vendor dataset is CC-BY-4.0.
  • Coverage. 532 of 703 domains (76%) returned readable HTML. The rest were bot-protected (HTTP 403), did not resolve, or refused connections.

Limitations, stated plainly

  • This measures signals, not compliance. We cannot see inside anyone's vendor console. A site running Zendesk with the AI agent switched off has no Article 50(1) duty, and our scan cannot tell the difference. "No disclosure wording found" is evidence of a gap worth checking, not a finding of breach.
  • The frame skews away from e-commerce. Tranco's upper ranks are dominated by news, banking, government and infrastructure. The 4.9% widget rate is therefore a floor for the general web, and almost certainly understates online retail, where chat widgets are far more common.
  • Disclosure detection is a proxy. We search visible page copy. Wording that appears only inside the chat window after it opens will be missed, so the 92.3% figure may overstate the true gap.
  • Bot protection is not random. The 171 unreadable domains skew toward larger sites, which likely have more compliance resource. This may bias findings pessimistically.
  • We sell a product in this space. Treat the framing accordingly, and check the raw data — it is published precisely so you do not have to take our word for it.
