# EU AI Act chatbot disclosure: fines, penalties, and what year-one enforcement really looks like

If your online shop runs an AI chatbot or AI agent and visitors aren't told it's AI, the EU AI Act gives regulators a serious number to point at — up to €15,000,000 or 3% of your worldwide annual turnover. The number is real, but it's a ceiling, not a price tag. This guide explains what Article 50 requires, which article sets the fine, who enforces it, and what a proportionate response actually costs.

## What Article 50 requires, in one paragraph

Article 50 of the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) is the transparency chapter. Its first duty: a provider must design AI systems that interact directly with people so that the person is told they're dealing with an AI system, clearly and no later than the first interaction — unless it's obvious from the circumstances. For an online shop, the duty in practice lands on the deployer: the business running the chat widget. If an AI mode answers customers on your site, the disclosure has to appear in the conversation itself, not buried in a privacy policy.

## Which article sets the fine

Transparency breaches are covered by Article 99(4) of the AI Act, which cites the transparency obligations in Article 50 [CHECK: confirm the exact sub-point — most consolidations show this as Article 99(4)(g)]. The ceiling is €15,000,000 or, for a company, 3% of total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year — whichever is higher.

The Act groups infringements into three bands: the highest (up to €35,000,000 or 7%) covers prohibited practices and serious high-risk failings; the middle band (up to €15,000,000 or 3%) covers most operator obligations, including Article 50 transparency; the lowest (up to €7,500,000 or 1%) covers supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to authorities. Three qualifiers matter for a small business:

- The Act requires penalties to be effective, proportionate and dissuasive, and to take the operator's size and situation into account. [CHECK: verify whether Article 99(6) caps fines for SMEs and start-ups at the lower of the two figures, or only requires size to be considered.]
- It's a ceiling, not a tariff. Actual amounts are set in each member state's national law.
- No fixed amount is written into the Act for "an unlabelled chatbot." €15m / 3% is the maximum band, not what a small shop will be billed.

## Who enforces it

Enforcement is national. Each member state designates one or more market surveillance authorities for the AI Act, coordinated at EU level. [CHECK: don't name a specific authority without confirming it's that country's AI Act competent authority — designations were still being finalised in several member states through 2025–2026; for example, Germany was moving a draft AI Market Surveillance Act.] Anyone can file a complaint — a customer, a consumer association, or a competitor — and it lands with the authority where you operate or sell.

## What enforcement realistically looks like in the first years

New EU digital rules follow a consistent arc. Early enforcement is complaint-driven and correction-first: a letter or information request asking you to fix the gap and show what you did, with fines reserved for refusal, repeat offences, or bad faith. For a shop, the realistic exposure isn't a surprise €15m invoice. It's a deadline scramble, a paper-trail problem ("we fixed it" lands better with dated evidence that you checked), and a trust problem when the complaint is public. The difference between a five-minute reply and an expensive one is dated evidence — a record of what you checked, what you changed, and when, kept before anyone asks.

## Why "the vendor handles it" is wrong

Chat platforms ship AI modes and disclosure settings; they don't discharge your duty. Article 50 places the obligation on the deployer — the business whose site the visitor is talking to. Whether the AI mode is switched on, and whether the disclosure actually appears, is your configuration. A vendor contract doesn't transfer the duty.

## FAQ

**Q: What's the fine for not disclosing an AI chatbot?**

A: Non-compliance with Article 50's transparency duties sits under Article 99(4) — administrative fines up to €15,000,000 or 3% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher. That's the ceiling; real amounts are set nationally and must be proportionate. [CHECK: exact sub-point reference.]

**Q: Who enforces Article 50?**

A: National market surveillance authorities, designated per member state and coordinated at EU level. Complaints land with the authority where you operate. [CHECK: examples.]

**Q: Will a small shop really be fined in 2026?**

A: Not typically as a first contact. Early enforcement is complaint-driven and correction-first — a letter before a fine. The likely near-term cost of a gap is a compliance scramble, not an immediate €15m fine.

**Q: My chat vendor says it's compliant. Am I covered?**

A: No. The disclosure duty is on the deployer — you. A compliant-capable product doesn't discharge your duty to actually disclose to your own visitors.

**Q: What should I do first?**

A: Check whether an AI mode answers customers, make the disclosure appear in the chat (every language you sell in), and keep dated evidence of what you changed and when.
